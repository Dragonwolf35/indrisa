# Description
Goblins are small, vicious humanoids who are acutely aware they're weak compared to most other races. This doesn't stop their ferocity however, it simply fuels their thirst to conquer others and subject them to the goblin's cruel ways. All those low on the societal rank, which includes some goblins, will have to serve the higher goblins like slaves. Those who prove to be more useful may be treated more like a pet, but goblins don't know kindness for pets the same way many other races do.

Like other goblinoids, goblins often had a short temper, and were more easily provoked than individuals of most other races. They often found it difficult to overcome this short fuse, and had a sense of greed that made it difficult for them to act altruistically. They also generally took sadistic pleasure in exacting revenge once crossed.

Despite their generally poor reputation however, not all goblins were dim-witted or evil. Some goblins have risen to become heroes, gaining enough renown to be accepted into the civilized world of other, more commonly good races. Those goblins seeking this path may have found it difficult to overcome their temper and greed, as well as the cultural influence of their brethren, but those who did often found it could be more rewarding, in the long run at least, to serve good rather than to serve evil. Those that did often made use of their ill-gained talents as rogues or fighters.

# Cultural Quirks
Because goblins are small and historically survive by scavenging the cast-offs of larger, more dangerous societies, they have developed a deep cultural mistrust of anything brand new. To a goblin, an object that is clean, shiny, and unblemished hasn't been "tested" by the world yet, meaning it is inherently dangerous, unreliable, or cursed.

Goblins absolutely refuse to use pristine items. If they buy a brand-new dagger from a blacksmith, they will immediately drag it across a stone floor to scratch the blade in front of the horrified smith. If given a newly tailored shirt, they will tear a small hole in the hem or rub dirt on the shoulder before putting it on.

In mixed company, a goblin merchant will enthusiastically charge you _more_ for a rusty, heavily dented helmet than a masterwork steel one, arguing that the dented one "already knows how to survive a hit." If an adventurer wants to show genuine affection and give a goblin a gift, they must present it heavily scuffed or lightly broken; handing a goblin something perfectly clean and wrapped is viewed as an attempted assassination.
