> [!narration] A New Adventure Begins
> Thousands of years ago, the sky didn't just fall — it shattered. The continent of Indrisa was broken, blown apart before it was reassembled again. But Indrisa was luckier than the rest — it at least remains.
> 
> The old gods were annihilated in a single, catastrophic blast; rumours and conspiracies about the explosion's origin have filled the heads and tongues of every citizen since, but all they know is that the world they knew is gone, leaving behind a continent-sized scar in the centre of the world. Now, reality itself is a fractured mosaic. Walk a few miles and a boiling desert violently gives way to a sub-zero taiga, or a claustrophobic black-water marsh bleeds into towering iron mountains. At the heart of it all lies [[Places/Regions/Boundary-End\|Boundary-End]], a churning, apocalyptic rim where the Elemental Planes constantly war for dominance. You live in the aftermath of a broken cosmos millennia later, a world held together by the lingering tension of dead gods and the hostile geography they left behind.
> 
> But out here on the western outskirts, the apocalypse usually feels like a distant rumour. It is a quiet frontier, a place where the biggest headache is typically a territorial dragon throwing a tantrum because a merchant wagon rolled too close to its lair. People here live simple, gruelling lives, trying their best to ignore the primal elemental horrors bleeding out from the continent's centre.
> 
> That quiet ended three days ago.
> 
> With a sickening groan of shifting earth and ozone, a dungeon violently tore its way up through the bedrock. Dungeons bursting from planar rifts are a fact of life in this era, but this one is entirely wrong. Despite having only existed in this world for seventy-two hours, its stone is crumbling with impossible age, and the air around it hums with a decaying, ancient magic that tastes like copper and stale blood. A a rotting, decrepit ruin that has just arrived.
> 
> Naturally, the local adventuring guilds descended like vultures. Which brings us to you.
> 
> You are not the legendary heroes clad in shimmering plate armour, nor are you the arrogant archmagi's looking to claim a dead god's relic. You are the hired help. You are the porters, the torch-bearers, and the pack mules, paid a handful of silver to manage the menial labour. Your job is to carry the heavy canvas sacks, drag the rations, and keep your heads down while the _real_ adventurers breach the decaying doors of this impossible ruin.
> 
> What is the heavy, embarrassing, or utterly useless piece of gear your assigned "hero" has forced you to carry as you all step into the dark?
