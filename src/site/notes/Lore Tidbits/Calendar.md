The world operates on a rigid, 512-day cycle built entirely on perfect eighths: 8 days to a week, 8 weeks to a month, and 8 months to a year. To scholars, peasants, and long-lived elves alike, this is not a cultural construct—it is the immutable law of nature.

Because the calendar dictates the rhythm of reality, the number eight is considered the foundation of stability. Architecture frequently features octagonal courtyards, poetry favours eight-syllable meters, and contracts are bound by eight-point signatures. To suggest the year could be divided any differently would sound as absurd to a commoner as suggesting water should fall upward.

###### The 8 Days of the Week
The days dictate the rhythm of labour and rest in frontier towns and capital cities alike:
- **Galeday:** The traditional start of the workweek, associated with fresh starts and travel.
- **Cragday:** A day for heavy labour, construction, and mining.
- **Emberday:** The mid-week point, often associated with forge-work and military training.
- **Tideday:** The primary market day for trading and shipping.
- **Frostday:** A day of rationing and preservation; historically considered unlucky for starting new ventures.
- **Slagday:** The hardest day of the workweek, reserved for undesirable chores and refuse clearing.
- **Mireday:** A slow day of administrative duties, bookkeeping, and planting.
- **Ashday:** The day of rest. Hearths are swept, and communities gather to share meals and recuperate.

###### The 8 Months of the Year
The months track the harsh, elemental shifts of the continent's seasons:

1. **Breath:** Harsh, howling spring winds that clear the winter smog.
2. **Mire:** The wet season of endless mud, sudden floods, and frantic planting.
3. **Tide:** Early summer, marked by clear skies, open sailing, and the safest overland travel.
4. **Forge:** Mid-summer, defined by stagnant air and punishing, relentless heat.
5. **Hearth:** Late summer; the great harvest season and the time for major festivals.
6. **Cinder:** Autumn; the air cools, the skies turn grey, and farmers burn away the dead crops.
7. **Stone:** Early winter; the ground freezes rock-solid, bringing travel to a grinding halt.
8. **Frost:** Deep winter; blinding snows, isolation, and strict survival rationing.
