# Description
Bugbears are terrifying, humanoid monsters with only three main pastimes: sleeping, eating and bullying other creatures into taking care of everything else they might need. Because of their more ferocious nature, their rarity and their monstrous appearance, bugbears have become part of many tales and legends of many other races. It's even said bugbears will cut off heads of their enemies and trap their souls within them.

They took their name from their noses and claws, which were similar to those of bears. Their claws were not long and sharp enough to be used as weapons, so bugbears often armoured and armed themselves with a variety of purloined gear, which was normally second-rate and in poor repair.

Bugbears, like other goblinoids, had a reputation for being dim-witted and brutish. This claim was not unfounded, and like their kin, bugbears had easily provoked tempers and were prone to rages. Few bugbears overcame this flaw and their culture's brutal nature. Many bugbears were chaotic evil in alignment, favouring stealth and surprising their opponents.

Bugbear heroes, though rare, were heard of and could acquire significant renown if successful. Often the motivation for this change of heart came from the rewards earned from virtue, which in the long term were more pleasing than the short-term pleasures of evil behaviours.

# Cultural Quirks

Despite their massive size, Bugbears are unnervingly stealthy, and they have adapted this predatory trait into their rules of social etiquette.

If a Bugbear is embarrassed, wants to avoid a conversation, or simply desires a nap, they will stand perfectly still in the shadows of a room or crouch behind a remarkably small piece of furniture. In Bugbear culture, it is considered a massive, unforgivable faux pas to acknowledge or speak to a hiding Bugbear. They are treating you to the courtesy of their "absence," and you must politely pretend they are not there.

When traveling in mixed company, non-Bugbear allies must constantly run interference to uphold this absurdity. If a 300-pound Bugbear gets bored during a tense negotiation and "hides" by holding a single decorative candlestick in front of their face, the party must deadpan explain to the confused nobles that their companion has left the room. If an uneducated outsider accidentally makes eye contact with or speaks directly to the hiding Bugbear, the Bugbear views it as a horrific invasion of privacy and may demand an immediate duel to restore their honour.
