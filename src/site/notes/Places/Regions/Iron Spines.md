# Description
The Iron Spines pierce the sky like jagged, soot-stained teeth, their peaks constantly veiled in a choking haze of ash and sulphur. Deep within this treacherous, rumbling range lies the continent's largest dwarven settlement, a hidden bastion carved directly into the stone beside a bizarre, unyielding forest of Iron Wood. Navigating these crags is a terrifying gamble against the earth itself; the ground frequently trembles and splits as sudden volcanic eruptions paint the slopes in liquid fire, leaving travellers to wonder if the cataclysms are mere geological disasters, or the restless stirring of colossal, ancient beasts waking in the deep.

## Seasonal Effect
### Spring — The Thaw
Deceptively dangerous. The warming temperatures trigger massive, unpredictable avalanches and flash-melt waterfalls that wash out mountain passes. Precipitation is a miserable mix of freezing rain, sleet, and heavy, wet snow. The wind constantly funnels through the valleys.

### Summer — The Brief Relief
The only time the high passes are reliably clear of ice. The days are crisp and comfortably warm, though temperatures still plummet near freezing at night. Precipitation comes as intense, localized afternoon thunderstorms that get trapped in the valleys, creating booming echoes that sound like rockslides.

### Autumn — The Early Freeze
Winter arrives early in the Iron Spines. The ambient temperature drops rapidly, and the winds turn sharp and biting, scouring the peaks. Snow begins falling heavily by the month of Cinder, quickly burying higher elevations and forcing wildlife down into the valleys.

### Winter — The Whiteout
Absolute hostility. Heavy, blinding snowstorms dump feet of powder at a time, completely erasing trails. Hurricane-force winds howl through the peaks, creating lethal wind chills and massive snow cornices. Without a guide and proper gear, traveling the Spines in winter is practically a death sentence.
